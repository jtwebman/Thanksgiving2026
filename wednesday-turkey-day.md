# Wednesday 11/25: Turkey Day (plus Frog Eye Salad Part 1)

**Plan on 3½–4 hours total.** Roasting takes about 1–1¼ hours of that, uncovered at 400°F.

**The plan:** cut both turkeys into pieces, inject them with butter-broth, rub them, and roast them in pans with broth. Then slice the meat and store it in its juices overnight. Turkey reheated in its own juices comes out much juicier than a whole bird that sat out.

## What you need
- 2 turkeys (10–12 lb each), thawed
- About 6 sticks of butter
- 2½ cartons low-sodium chicken broth (about 10 cups): injection, pans, and gravy
- ½ cup flour (for the gravy)
- 2 onions, 4 celery ribs, 1 head garlic, and fresh herbs (optional)
- Rub and injection spices (below)
- 2 full-size roasting pans (1 per turkey), set on baking sheets so they don't bend
- Meat injector, oven-safe meat thermometer, heavy-duty foil, a sharp knife, and kitchen shears

---

## 1. Check the turkeys and prep them (15 min)
1. **Thaw check:** the cavity should have no ice, and the legs should wiggle freely at the joints. If the turkeys are still icy, put them breast-down in a sink of **cold** water for 30–60 min, changing the water every 30 min.
2. Pull out the **neck** (in the main cavity) and the **giblet bag** (often tucked under the neck skin flap). Toss them, or save them for broth.
3. **Pat the turkeys very dry** with paper towels. Dry skin takes the rub better and browns better.
4. Set the oven rack positions for 2 racks: one in the lower third and one in the upper third.

## 2. Break down the turkeys (about 15 min each)
Do this breast-side up on a big cutting board. Gloves help.
1. **Legs:** pull a leg away from the body and slice through the skin between the leg and the breast. Bend the leg back until the hip joint pops, then cut through the joint. Repeat on the other side.
2. **Split each leg** into a **thigh** and a **drumstick** by cutting through the joint. Feel for the line of fat on the underside; that's where the joint is.
3. **Wings:** pull each wing out and cut through the shoulder joint.
4. **Boneless breasts (leave the skin on):** find the breastbone running down the center. Cut down along one side of it, keeping the knife tight against the bone. Then use short strokes along the rib cage to peel the breast half away from the bones, pulling it back gently with your other hand as you go. Cut through any skin left at the edges. Repeat on the other side.
5. **Carcass:** whatever's left goes in the freezer for soup, or in the trash.

**From both turkeys you'll have:** 4 boneless skin-on breast halves, 4 bone-in thighs, 4 drumsticks, and 4 wings.

## 3. Butter injection (10 min to make, 15 min to inject)
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Butter, melted | 3 sticks (1½ cups) |
| Low-sodium chicken broth, warm | 1½ cups |
| Kosher salt | 2 Tbsp Diamond Crystal (or 1 Tbsp + 1 tsp Morton) |
| Garlic powder | 2 tsp |
| Onion powder | 1 tsp |

1. Heat the broth, stir in the salt until it dissolves, then add the melted butter and powders. **Keep it warm** (lowest burner setting) so the butter doesn't harden in the needle. If the needle clogs, strain the mix.
2. **How much goes where (per turkey, about 1½ cups):**
   - Each breast half: about ½ cup, spread over 6–8 spots
   - Each thigh: 2–3 Tbsp
   - Each drumstick: 1–2 Tbsp
   - Wings: skip them
3. **How to inject:** push the needle deep, then press the plunger slowly while you pull the needle out. Use the same entry hole and angle it in different directions so the skin doesn't get torn up. Some leakage is normal; let it run into the pan.

## 4. Rub (10 min)
Mix in a bowl:
| Spice | Amount |
|---|---|
| Kosher salt | 1 Tbsp |
| Black pepper | 1 Tbsp |
| Garlic powder | 1 Tbsp |
| Onion powder | 1 Tbsp |
| Smoked paprika | 1 Tbsp |
| Brown sugar | 1 Tbsp |
| Dried thyme | 2 tsp |
| Rubbed sage | 2 tsp |
| Dried rosemary, crushed | 1 tsp |

1. Melt **1 stick of butter** and brush it all over the pieces.
2. Sprinkle the rub on all sides. Loosen the breast skin with your fingers and push some rub **under** the skin too.

## 5. Set up the pans (1 per turkey)
Preheat the oven to **400°F**. Set the racks in the lower third and upper third, **1 pan per rack**.

In **each pan**, scatter 1 onion (quartered), 2 celery ribs (in chunks), 6 smashed garlic cloves, and a few herb sprigs. The vegetables act as a rack for the turkey. Add **1½ cups broth** and **4 Tbsp butter** in pats. The broth keeps the drippings from burning at 400°F, and those drippings become the gravy.

**Arrange each turkey's pieces skin-side up, with the 2 breast halves in the middle and the thighs, drumsticks, and wings around the edges.** The edges of the pan get hotter, and the dark meat needs to cook longer, so that's where it goes. It's fine if the pieces fit snugly.

## 6. Roast uncovered at 400°F (about 1–1¼ hours)
Pieces this size don't need foil. Roasting uncovered browns the skin and makes better drippings.

1. Roast **uncovered**, 1 pan per rack.
2. **At 25 min:** swap the pans between racks (top to bottom), baste with the pan juices, and **start checking the breasts**. If a pan looks dry, add ½ cup water so the drippings don't burn.
3. **Pull each piece when it's done.** Don't wait for the whole pan.
   - **Boneless breasts: pull at 145–150°F** (thickest part), usually around **30–40 min**. Rest them loosely tented with foil. They need to **stay at 145°F or above for 10 minutes**, and they'll usually climb a few degrees while they rest, so that takes care of itself. Your oven-safe thermometer can stay in to confirm it.
   - **Wings: pull when they're browned and at 175°F or above**, usually around the same time as the breasts.
   - **Thighs and drumsticks: pull at 180–185°F**, usually **1–1¼ hours**. At that temperature the connective tissue breaks down and the dark meat gets tender.

**Why 145°F is safe:** poultry held at 145°F for about 10 minutes is as safe as poultry brought to 165°F for an instant, and it stays much juicier. The breast also gets reheated to 165°F tomorrow, so taking it out lower today keeps it from overcooking.

> **Using your oven-safe thermometer:** leave it in the **thickest breast half**, since the breast is what dries out, and it lets you watch the 10-minute hold at 145°F. To check the dark meat, move it into the thickest thigh (not touching bone) and wait about a minute for the needle to stop moving. Because it can only be in one piece at a time, check the other pans' pieces once each too.

## 7. Slice, pack, and refrigerate (30–45 min)
1. Rest the pieces for **30 min**. Don't leave cooked turkey out for more than **2 hours** total.
2. **Breasts:** slice them about ½" thick, across the grain.
3. **Thighs:** keep them whole, or pull the meat off the bone. **Drumsticks and wings:** leave them whole.
4. **Strain the pan juices** through a colander into a big bowl. Toss the vegetables.
5. **Set aside up to 3 cups of the juices for gravy** (step 8). Uncovered roasting makes fewer drippings. If you have less, the gravy makes up the difference with broth.
6. Scrape out the 2 roasting pans and pack the turkey back into them: sliced breast in one, dark meat in the other. Leave the browned bits on the bottom; they add flavor. **Pour the rest of the juices over the meat.** This is what keeps it juicy. Thursday's reheat adds more broth, so it's fine if the meat isn't fully covered.
7. Cover tightly with foil and refrigerate.

## 8. Turkey Gravy (20 min, makes about 6 cups)
Making the gravy today means one less thing on Thursday, and gravy reheats perfectly.

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Strained turkey drippings (from step 7) | up to 3 cups |
| Fat skimmed from the drippings, topped up with butter if short | ½ cup |
| Flour | ½ cup |
| Low-sodium chicken broth | enough to make **6 cups of liquid total** with the drippings |
| Black pepper | ½ tsp |
| Salt | **only after tasting** |

1. **Separate the fat:** let the drippings sit for 5–10 min. The fat rises to the top. Spoon it off into a measuring cup. If you get less than ½ cup of fat, add butter to make ½ cup.
2. **Roux:** melt the fat in a medium saucepan over medium heat. Whisk in the flour and cook it, whisking, for **2–3 min**, until it's golden and smells nutty.
3. **Add the liquid slowly:** whisk in the drippings and broth **about 1 cup at a time**. Whisk out the lumps after each cup before you add the next.
4. **Simmer for 5–10 min**, whisking now and then, until it coats a spoon.
5. **Taste it before you add any salt.** The drippings are already salty from the injection and rub, so you may not need any. Add the pepper (and a pinch of dried thyme or sage if you like).
6. **Fixes:**
   - Too thick: whisk in more broth.
   - Too thin: simmer it longer, or whisk in 1 Tbsp cornstarch stirred into 2 Tbsp cold water.
   - Lumpy: pour it through a strainer.
7. Let it cool, then refrigerate it in a covered container. It will set into a jelly in the fridge. That's normal.

## Frog Eye Salad, Part 1 (about 45 min, any time Wednesday)
The pasta and custard need to **chill overnight**.

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Acini di pepe pasta | 1 lb |
| Sugar | 1 cup |
| Flour | 2 Tbsp |
| Salt | ½ tsp |
| Eggs, beaten | 2 |
| Pineapple juice (from the cans, plus backup) | 1¾ cups |
| Lemon juice | 1 Tbsp |

1. **Open and drain the 2 cans of pineapple**, saving the juice. Measure out 1¾ cups, topping it up with the backup juice if needed. Put the pineapple in a covered container in the fridge for tomorrow.
2. **Custard:** in a saucepan, whisk together the sugar, flour, and salt. Whisk in the pineapple juice and eggs. Cook over **medium heat, stirring constantly**, until it thickens and bubbles (5–8 min). Stir in the lemon juice and let it cool.
3. **Pasta:** boil it in salted water according to the package (about 9–11 min). Drain it and rinse with cold water until it's cool. Drain it well.
4. Stir the custard into the pasta in a **big bowl** (it's going to get huge tomorrow). Cover and **refrigerate overnight**.
5. Move the **Cool Whip from the freezer to the fridge** so it's thawed by tomorrow.
